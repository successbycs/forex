# M20.12 — multi-timeframe context trial for M1 Demo execution

## Purpose

M20.12 is a small, Demo-only expansion of the M1 trading MVP.  It keeps M1
as the only entry, order, stop-loss, take-profit, and trade-management
timeframe.  Closed candles from selected higher timeframes are recorded as
**read-only context** so the team can measure whether they improve the quality
of M1 decisions.

This is not an assertion that multiple-timeframe analysis creates a trading
edge.  It is an instrumented experiment.  No M20.12 change may access a Live
server, change the active Demo order cap, create a second position, or grant a
higher timeframe independent order authority.

## Research basis and limitation

Research supports treating price behaviour, volatility, liquidity, trading
hours, and transaction costs as horizon-dependent rather than assuming an M1
signal is universally meaningful:

- Elaut, Frömmel, and Lampaert, *Intraday Momentum in FX Markets* (2018),
  finds intraday momentum related to liquidity provision and trading hours in
  its studied FX market: [university record](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/8060014).
- Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen, *Time Series Momentum* (2012), documents
  longer-horizon momentum across liquid currency futures, but does not prove
  an M1 EURUSD rule: [NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25551.pdf).
- Raza, Marshall, and Visaltanachoti, *Is There Momentum or Reversal in
  Weekly Currency Returns?* (2014), shows that weekly FX results vary with
  horizon, volatility, and transaction costs: [SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2258253).
- Karnaukh, Ranaldo, and Söderlind, *Liquidity in the Global Currency Market*
  (2022), links FX price impact to transaction costs, uncertainty, and risk
  aversion: [article record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2022.09.004).

The resulting design is a controlled measurement plan, not financial advice
or a profitability claim.  Retail FX leverage and dealer execution remain
material risks; see the [CFTC forex advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/CustomerAdvisory_MustKnowForex.html).

## Fixed authority boundary

| Layer | Timeframe | M20.12 role | May submit or close an order? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry / owner | M1 | Existing five-strategy selection, entry, SL, TP, and owner exit. | Yes — unchanged, subject to the existing Demo-only contract. |
| Setup quality | M5 | Measure immediate continuation or failure around the M1 set-up. | No. |
| Intraday regime | H1 | Classify trend, range, mixed, or unavailable context. | No. |
| Later structure extension | H4 / D1 | Deferred until M5/H1 data quality and measurement justify a deterministic structure rule. | No. |
| Later slow map | W1 | Deferred weekly reporting context only; never part of the fast listener path. | No. |

`GOMarketsMU-Demo`, `EURUSD`, M1 execution, one open position, the continuous
Demo lease, proposal-before-attempt, broker-side SL/TP, and append-only audit
remain mandatory.  W1 is deliberately a slow read-only map; it is not a
weekly order strategy.

## Version 1 decision model — M5/H1 shadow context only

```text
fresh Demo quote + closed M1 candles
  -> existing M1 safety gates and deterministic strategy selection
  -> retrieve only the fixed closed M5/H1 context bars
  -> classify context as ALIGNED, NEUTRAL, OPPOSED, or UNAVAILABLE
  -> persist the context with the M1 proposal and assessment
  -> M1 owner executes or records NO_TRADE under the unchanged M20 rules
```

M20.12 v1 must not require all timeframes to agree, block an otherwise valid
M1 order, or alter an M1 exit.  Requiring universal alignment would
over-filter a short-horizon system and hide the experiment's effect.  Its
overall `context_disposition` is therefore `OBSERVE_ONLY`, `NEUTRAL`, or
`HARD_CONFLICT`, but the disposition is **not authority** in v1.  `UNAVAILABLE`
is a visible data-quality condition, normally recorded as `NEUTRAL/OBSERVE_ONLY`
rather than creating a full entry outage.

Only a later, explicitly contracted Demo-only experiment, after
broker-matched comparison evidence and an explicit operator decision, may test
one change at a time: either an H1 hard-conflict entry block or a separately
versioned holding-time rule.  It is not assumed to be an M20 work package;
it belongs on the M27-or-later governed pathway.  H4/D1 structure cannot
become a block until its lookback, zone width, distance-to-target, tie cases,
and exact reason are deterministic and tested.  Each rule version,
closed-candle time, retrieval time, calculation inputs, and reason must be
persisted; no discretionary override is permitted.

## Holding-time policy

M20.12 v1 does **not** alter the owner-specific M1 maximum holds (currently
six to ten minutes).  Context is first measured against the unchanged exit
contract.  This avoids confusing a context filter with a new holding-period
strategy.

Only a later, separately versioned Demo experiment may test a longer hold,
and only for the explicitly defined `ALIGNED` context cohort.  It must retain the original broker-side
SL, use a pre-declared maximum (not an extension because a trade is losing),
and report separately by owner, context class, exit reason, costs, and realised
AUD P&L.  `HARD_CONFLICT` never justifies a longer hold.

## Minimum data and audit additions

For every M1 assessment, persist one immutable M5/H1 context record containing:

- timeframe and latest closed-candle timestamp;
- data age and integrity result;
- state: `BULLISH`, `BEARISH`, `RANGE`, `MIXED`, or `UNKNOWN`;
- volatility/liquidity state derived from the fixed closed-candle inputs and
  the existing observed spread;
- alignment with the selected M1 side: `ALIGNED`, `NEUTRAL`, `OPPOSED`, or
  `NOT_APPLICABLE`;
- deterministic rule version and human-readable reason.

The proposal/selection record then stores the overall context class and its
reason.  It must also retain the M1 proposal that would have been executable
before context so the analysis can compare context buckets without pretending
that an untraded shadow candidate has a realised broker outcome.  The existing
ledger and strategy-trial dashboard must later be able to group countable
broker outcomes by owner, M1 regime, context class, and rule version.  Raw tick
streams, a new analytics platform, a second listener, and portfolio
optimisation are explicitly out of scope.

## Work-package deliverables

1. Add fixed, closed-candle M5/H1 retrieval to the existing
   Demo-only runner without an arbitrary timeframe or symbol parameter.
2. Define and test deterministic states, alignment, native-candle freshness,
   point-in-time alignment, and `OBSERVE_ONLY` classification.  A new M1 tick
   must not recalculate an H1 conclusion before the next H1 close.
3. Add the smallest append-only PostgreSQL schema needed for immutable context
   records and proposal-level context classification.
4. Keep the listener dashboard readable: show selected M1 owner first, then a
   compact M5/H1 context summary, candle-close timestamps, ages, and the exact
   statement `Shadow context only — does not change this trade.`
5. Keep the ledger and trial scoreboard able to compare broker-reconciled
   results by owner and context class.  Rejected/unverified records remain
   separate.
6. Deploy only through the current hash-checked T480 release path and prove
   the same Demo-only server, one-position guard, caps, proposal-first audit,
   and read-only dashboards still hold.

## Acceptance evidence

- Tests demonstrate that M1 remains the sole execution authority and that a
  non-M1 context can neither reserve nor close a position.
- Tests reject forming, wrong-symbol, wrong-server, or internally inconsistent
  context; unavailable M5/H1 data is visibly recorded without silently
  changing M1 order authority.
- A fresh Demo assessment stores every fixed context row with the M1 decision
  snapshot and a single deterministic classification.
- At least one broker-reconciled M1 lifecycle is queryable with its immutable
  context class; no profitability conclusion is required.
- The release, revision, configuration fingerprint, lease, context schema,
  dashboard result, and PostgreSQL audit are bound in fresh M20 evidence.

## Demo-to-Live execution roadmap — M27 and later

This is a planning and governance map, not an implementation authority.  It
does not add a server, credential, runtime configuration, account connection,
or order route.  Every step through M32 remains on `GOMarketsMU-Demo` and
`EURUSD`; `GOMarketsMU-Live` is prohibited.  M20, M20.11, and M20.12 cannot
satisfy, accelerate, or bypass any gate below.

| Stage | Required outcome before moving on | What it explicitly does not authorise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M20.12 shadow observation | Immutable M1/M5/H1 records are collected while the existing M1 decision and exit rules remain unchanged. Outcomes are analysed by context bucket, including missing/stale data. | Context filtering, changed holding time, Live access, or a profitability claim. |
| M27–M29 Demo operational validation | The existing contracts prove fresh Demo data, tick/spread collection, and interruption/restart recovery with their declared evidence and review gates. | A broader execution surface or Live-readiness approval. |
| M30–M31 controlled Demo execution and evaluation | Broker-matched Demo lifecycles, fee/cost reconciliation, pre-declared baseline comparison, and operational outcomes are retained and independently inspectable. | A claim that Demo fills or P&L transfer to Live. |
| M32 forward Demo assessment | Retained forward Demo evidence is assessed under its Review Board gate. The assessment identifies gaps and a recommendation, if any. | Live connectivity or authority: the M32 contract itself grants neither. |
| M33-or-later, separately proposed Live-readiness programme | Only after a new human-approved contract exists may the project assess a tightly bounded Live-readiness design. It must be separately implemented, tested, evidenced, reviewed, and explicitly authorised. | Automatic promotion from Demo, reuse of Demo credentials, or unattended Live activation. |

### Mandatory gates for any later Live-readiness proposal

All gates are cumulative.  Passing one is evidence for that gate alone, not a
permission to enable Live.  A later contract must make the exact thresholds,
cohort, rule versions, review roles, and current evidence freshness explicit.

1. **Sustained, pre-declared Demo evidence.**  Retain a sufficiently long and
   representative forward cohort across the strategy, session, regime, and
   M5/H1 context buckets. Compare realised results with the pre-declared
   baseline; include no-trades, rejects, outages, and context-unavailable
   records. Do not select a threshold after inspecting the outcome.
2. **Broker reconciliation and costs.**  Reconcile every attempted and closed
   Demo transaction from proposal through broker history. Account for realised
   P&L, commission, swap, spread/slippage or other estimated costs, exit
   reason, holding time, and unresolved/missing records. A reconciliation gap
   fails the gate; gross P&L alone is insufficient.
3. **Risk and exposure controls.**  Independently verify bounded loss,
   position, session, instrument, sizing, stop, kill/pause, and concentration
   limits. Demonstrate the controls fail closed and that no configuration can
   broaden the approved authority by accident.
4. **Operational monitoring and recovery.**  Demonstrate observable health,
   alerting, ledger integrity, idempotency, restart/outage recovery, and a
   human-operated pause/rollback procedure on the declared Demo surface.
   Notification delivery is supplementary; it cannot replace reconciliation
   or control monitoring.
5. **Security and separation review.**  Perform a fresh threat, credential,
   least-privilege, secret-handling, audit-retention, and deployment review.
   Demo and any proposed Live credentials, configuration, service identity,
   and release authorization must remain separate. No credential is stored in
   Git or evidence.
6. **Human and governance authorisation.**  Obtain the contract-required
   review results and explicit human approval after the evidence is reviewed.
   A separate, written authorisation must name the exact approved future
   account surface, revision, configuration fingerprint, risk envelope,
   rollout plan, and emergency stop owner before any Live capability could be
   enabled. Absence of that authorisation means no Live action.

### Context graduation decision framework

The M20.12 data may support a later *Demo-only* experiment only when the
pre-declared comparison shows adequate coverage and data quality, and the
operator approves a new versioned contract.  The experimental cohort must
change **exactly one** variable from the matched shadow baseline:

- **Option A — H1 hard-entry filter:** reject a pre-existing executable M1
  candidate only for the exact, deterministic H1 `HARD_CONFLICT` condition.
  M1 stop, target, owner, and hold-time rules remain unchanged.
- **Option B — holding-time policy:** retain the existing entry eligibility and
  use a pre-declared, bounded holding-time rule for the stated context cohort.
  No H1 context filter is applied, and broker-side protection remains in place.

The two options must never run in the same cohort, release, rule version, or
comparison window.  Separate cohorts need immutable candidate-level records,
their shadow baseline, cost-inclusive outcomes, MAE/MFE where available,
holding time, exit reason, and the reason for inclusion or exclusion.  A
negative, inconclusive, stale-data, or safety result requires reverting to
observation-only; it does not justify discretionary tuning.

## Independent trader-domain review — 04/09/26

An independent AI trader-domain review returned **CONDITIONAL PASS** for the
smaller M5/H1 context-recording experiment, and rejected an immediate
hard-filter or holding-time change.  Its material findings have been adopted:

- keep M1 as the sole execution, ownership, and exit authority;
- account for intraday liquidity/trading-hour and realistic-cost sensitivity;
- persist native closed-candle times, retrieval times, inputs, and rule
  version to prevent look-ahead;
- compare frequency, net P&L, MAE/MFE, costs, holding time, exit reason, and
  session by context bucket; do not infer a filter benefit from a small
  selection of P&L rows;
- defer H4/D1/W1 fast-path use and any M1 hold-time extension; and
- preserve the current explicit `news_blackout_not_configured` limitation.

The review cited evidence that intraday technical-rule returns can disappear
after realistic costs and trading-hour constraints ([Neely & Weller,
2003](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0261-5606(02)00101-8);
[Brooks & Hinich, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9876.2006.00534.x)),
and that FX volatility/liquidity is intraday-seasonal ([Baillie & Bollerslev,
1991](https://doi.org/10.2307/2298012)).  It is an AI-assisted independent
review, not a regulated financial adviser opinion.
